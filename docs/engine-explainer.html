Read it left to right, top to bottom. The dashed box in the middle is the part that repeats — once for every single output token. Almost everything hard about this engine lives inside that box. Click any stage to filter the term matrix below to the things that live there.

The one asymmetry to keep in your head Prefill is compute-bound. Decode is memory-bound. They need opposite fixes, and mixing them up has cost this project real money. Prefill already scales — server-instrumented prefill rises 171.6 → 314.7 tok/s from B=1 to B=256 [measured]. Decode rises further and from much lower, and every hard problem below is a decode problem. Batching is the one lever that turns decode's memory-bound shape into prefill's compute-bound shape — which is why aggregate throughput and per-user throughput pull in opposite directions, and why you must always say which one you mean.

Sources. Every number traces to memory/mission/CEILINGS.json (physics bounds and the anti-pessimism protocol), memory/mission/FACTS.md (hardware-measured only, with provenance and retractions), the 55 memory/mission/wave*.md agent logs, ARC_V2.md, research/code/CODE_INDEX.md and docs/BENCHMARKS.md. [measured] = ran on real hardware. [physics] = a bound reality imposes, derived from bytes and bandwidth. [derived] = arithmetic on measured inputs. [projected] = arithmetic, not yet run. [measured-by-design] = the byte accounting is exact and verified in source, but the path has not executed on a GPU.

How this vocabulary was assembled. Not from a list somebody remembered. Four independent sweeps were run and merged: (1) the whole repo — all 85 files under docs/, every crate's modules and rustdoc, ARC_V2.md, arc-tools/; (2) the durable knowledge base — every memory/mission/wave*.md log plus FACTS, CEILINGS, DOCTRINE, BACKLOG and STATUS; (3) the published feature lists of nine competing engines — vLLM, SGLang, Modular MAX, LMDeploy, TensorRT-LLM, llama.cpp, ExLlamaV2/V3, TGI and DeepSpeed-FastGen — read for what they name, never for how fast they claim to be; (4) the 166-paper research corpus in research/, whose PDF filenames and code/ directory names are the technique names. Anything found in one sweep and missing from the page was added. That cross-check is the point of the exercise.

House rule, and it is the important one: a measured number describes today's code. It is never evidence about what is possible. Only physics bounds what is possible. Quote the gap and the bound in the same breath, so a bad number reads as work to do rather than as a verdict.

No competitor performance numbers appear on this page that we have not run ourselves. Naming what vLLM or SGLang have is fine; claiming how fast they are is not. Where a reference project's own published figure is cited — the 90.8 8-shot GSM8K, EXL3's ~80%-of-peak GEMV, SGLang's 2.30 MTP acceptance floor, colibrì's 71.6% routing predictability — it is labelled as theirs, and as unverified by us. One outstanding item: ARC_V2.md's target table still carries SGLang throughput figures we have never run. They are not reproduced here and should be struck from that file.
